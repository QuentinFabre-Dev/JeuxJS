For each commitment below, decide whether the deliverable honours it.

You are given excerpts of the deliverable, each prefixed by its sentence id.
These excerpts were selected for their vocabulary, so **absence from them is
weak evidence of absence from the document** — say `missing` when nothing
addresses the commitment, but keep your confidence low unless the deliverable
clearly should have mentioned it here.

Statuses:

- `met` — the deliverable does what was promised, and you can cite where.
- `partial` — it is addressed but incompletely: one system out of three, a
  section present but empty of substance, a date mentioned but not respected.
- `missing` — nothing in these excerpts addresses it.
- `contradicted` — the deliverable states or does the opposite. An excluded
  subject presented as delivered belongs here, not in `partial`.

Rules:

- **`evidence` holds sentence ids, never quotes.** An id the reader can click
  beats a paraphrase they have to go and find.
- Cite at most three ids: the ones that decide the verdict, not everything on
  the topic.
- `explanation` is one sentence written for the person who signs off before
  sending this to the client — what is wrong, or what proves it is right.
- A deliverable that mentions a subject without treating it is `partial`, not
  `met`. Being generous here is how this feature becomes useless.
