Read this signed statement of work and list what it **commits the firm to**.

A commitment is something a reader could later hold against the deliverable:

- `deliverable` — a document, a report, a workshop, a piece of software to hand over;
- `scope` — a subject, a perimeter, a population, a system that must be covered;
- `exclusion` — something explicitly out of scope, which the deliverable must
  therefore *not* claim to have done;
- `constraint` — a method, a standard, a framework that must be followed;
- `format` — a structure, a length, a language, a required section;
- `date` — a deadline or a milestone.

Rules:

- **Copy the clause verbatim into `quote`.** A commitment nobody can trace back
  to its sentence is a commitment nobody can argue about.
- One commitment per obligation. Do not merge two promises into one line, and
  do not split one promise into two.
- Ignore boilerplate: payment terms, liability caps, governing law, notice
  periods, confidentiality. They are real obligations, but not ones a
  deliverable can honour or break.
- `critical` is true only when failing the commitment breaks the contract
  rather than disappointing the client.
- If a clause is vague ("best efforts", "as appropriate"), still list it, and
  say in `text` what would count as honouring it.
